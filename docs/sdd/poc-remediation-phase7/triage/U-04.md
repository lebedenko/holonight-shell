| I-10 | `NetworkService::backend_` is double-owned via `unique_ptr` and `setParent(this)` | Not-a-bug | — | Not in the Phase 0-6 manifest — `NetworkService.{h,cpp}` untouched since the original review. Verified `backend_` is `std::unique_ptr<NetworkManagerBackend>` (`NetworkService.h:156`), additionally `setParent(this)`'d in the constructor (`NetworkService.cpp:144`) — the pattern is real, but re-examination shows it is a *repeated, codebase-wide idiom*, not an isolated mistake: a codebase-wide grep for `setParent(this)` (`libs/`, `apps/`) turns up the identical "owning `unique_ptr` member + `setParent(this)` in the constructor" construct for `IdleService::user_inhibitor_` (`std::unique_ptr<IdleInhibitor>`, `IdleService.h:76`) and `ScreenSaverAdaptor`'s `inhibitor_` (`std::unique_ptr<IdleInhibitor>`, `idle/ScreenSaverAdaptor.h:51`) — three independent, unrelated files consistently apply the same construct, which is the architectural signature of a deliberate convention (making an owned `QObject` discoverable via `QObject::children()`/parent-child tooling) rather than a one-off oversight in `NetworkService` specifically. It is also provably safe under the language's own guarantees, not merely "safe today by luck" as the original write-up hedged: C++ destroys derived-class members (the `unique_ptr`, hence `backend_`) before the base `~QObject()` runs its child-cleanup loop, and any `QObject`'s destructor unregisters itself from its parent's children list as its first action — so by the time `~QObject()` (the `NetworkService` parent) walks its children, the already-`unique_ptr`-destroyed `backend_` has already removed itself from that list; there is no execution order in which the base destructor's loop could double-delete it. This is the same structural guarantee that makes the identical pattern safe at the other two call sites, supporting a deliberate, safe, repeated idiom rather than a defect. |
